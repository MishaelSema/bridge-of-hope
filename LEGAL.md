# Legal basis

What the foundation is registered under, what governs its field of work, and
where each statement comes from. Every citation on the website should be
traceable to a primary text listed at the bottom of this file.

Checked on **17 November 2026**.

## The framework at a glance

| Question | Instrument |
|---|---|
| May the organisation exist? | Law No. 90/053 of 19 December 1990 on freedom of association |
| Is it an NGO? | Law No. 99/014 of 22 December 1999 governing NGOs |
| Who grants NGO approval, and how? | Decree No. 2001/150/PM of 3 May 2001 (Technical Commission) |
| Which ministry holds the file? | Ministry of Territorial Administration (**MINAT**) |
| What governs the mission — disability? | Law No. 2010/002 of 13 April 2010, applied by Decree No. 2018/6233/PM of 26 July 2018 |

## How the two statutes fit together

**Law No. 90/053 of 19 December 1990** is the general law on freedom of
association. It defines an association, sets the declaration regime, and
provides that a declaration plus two copies of the statutes confers legal
personality. Its Article 5(4) now provides that political parties, trade
unions, sports associations, **NGOs**, artistic and cultural associations and
interprofessional organisations are governed by *separate* texts. That
paragraph was inserted by **Law No. 2021/022 of 16 December 2021**, which
amended Article 5(4) of the 1990 law and nothing else. An earlier amendment,
**Law No. 2020/009 of 20 July 2020**, also touched the 1990 law.

**Law No. 99/014 of 22 December 1999** is that separate text for NGOs. Its
Article 1 states that NGOs are created and operate *within the framework of
the legislation on freedom of association and of this law* — which is why the
two are always cited together. The sequence it sets out:

1. The organisation must already be a **declared association** (or an
   authorised foreign association) with **at least three years of effective
   contribution** in a field of general interest — legal, economic, social,
   cultural, health, sports, education, humanitarian, environment or human
   rights (Articles 3 and 4).
2. It files an approval (*agrément*) application with the **Governor** of the
   region where it has its seat (Article 6).
3. The Governor forwards it within 15 days to the **Technical Commission**
   (Article 6(3)), which has 30 days to give a reasoned opinion (Article 8).
4. The **Minister of Territorial Administration** grants approval by order
   within 75 days; silence past that deadline is deemed approval (Article 9).
5. Approval confers legal personality, runs for **five years** and is
   renewable (Articles 9(3) and 10).

**Decree No. 2001/150/PM of 3 May 2001** organises the Technical Commission —
the body created on paper by Article 7 of the 1999 law and only constituted in
2001.

## The ministry is MINAT, not MINATD

The ministry was the Ministry of Territorial Administration and
Decentralisation (MINATD) from 2002 until 2018. Decree No. 2018/190 of
2 March 2018 moved decentralisation to a new ministry (MINDDEVEL) and the
Ministry of Territorial Administration reverted to its earlier name. Decree
No. 2019/030 of 23 January 2019 reorganised it as the **Ministry of
Territorial Administration (MINAT)**. NGO approval files sit in its
Directorate of Political Affairs, Sub-Directorate of Public Liberties, NGO
Service.

## What was wrong before

The site cited **Law No. 2010/011** as the NGO registration law. That number
belongs to a different instrument entirely: Law No. 2010/011 of 29 July 2010
amends the **Mining Code** (Law No. 2001/001 of 16 April 2001). It has nothing
to do with associations, NGOs or disability. The error is corrected in
`site/lib/i18n.ts`, `site/lib/legal.ts`, `site/lib/posts.ts`,
`site/lib/pages.ts` and `site/app/[lang]/about/page.tsx`.

The same citation had also drifted into two internal documents,
`PRODUCT.md` and `BRIDGE OF HOPE - BUSINESS PLAN.md`; both are corrected.

A second, separate confusion is worth naming: the disability statute is
**Law No. 2010/002 of 13 April 2010**, not 2010/011. It is the law that
guarantees access to education, employment, transport and public buildings,
and it is the correct instrument to cite for the foundation's *mission* — as
opposed to its *registration*.

## What the site now says

The footer and the About page carry a short line:

> Registered NGO, MINAT, under Law No. 99/014 of 22 December 1999.

The About page adds a legal status block naming all three instruments — NGO
approval, freedom of association, disability rights — with their exact
numbers and dates. The terms of use state the governing-law position in full.

## One thing to confirm

Law No. 99/014 reserves the word *NGO* for an association that has received
the ministerial *agrément*. An organisation that has only been **declared** at
the prefecture or divisional office — holding a *récépissé* under Law No.
90/053 — is a registered association, not yet an approved NGO.

The site says "Registered NGO". If what the foundation currently holds is the
declaration receipt rather than the ministerial approval order, the wording
should be "Registered association" until the *agrément* is issued. The
distinction matters to funders, who will ask for the order number.

## Keeping this current

Laws change. Before republishing a legal claim, re-check three things: whether
Law No. 99/014 has been amended, whether Article 5(4) of Law No. 90/053 has
been amended again, and whether the ministry has been renamed or reorganised.
The Presidency (<https://www.prc.cm>), the Prime Minister's services
(<https://www.spm.gov.cm>) and MINAT (<https://minat.gov.cm>) publish the
texts and the decrees of organisation.
